# Plan — 2026-08-30: the lexicon gate exempts our own quotes of the record, not just whole titles

*Status: DRAFT, awaiting operator approval — this is a proposed GUIDE §2
amendment plus its implementation, shaped per
[plan-task-template.md](plan-task-template.md). GUIDE changes precede
implementation (§10); nothing here is applied. No code has been
touched.*

## The incident that motivates this

Two nights running (2026-08-28 and 08-29's targets), the EOD finalizer
tripped the lexicon gate on the same word, "historic," in two different
bills — H.R. 1693 ("...the **National Historic** Trails Interpretive
Center...") and H.R. 8121 ("...the Christiansted **National Historic**
Site..."). Both are real, official place names: the site's legal name
contains the banned word "historic" as a proper-noun component, not as
an evaluative adjective. Both survived two corrective-rewrite attempts
(GUIDE §6 rule 14a) and were withdrawn — a disclosed gap, not a wrong
digest, but a real loss: two items the pipeline had already summarized
correctly, discarded because the gate could not tell "this bill
concerns the National Historic Trails Interpretive Center" (a fact)
from "this historic bill..." (editorializing).

## Root cause: the exemption requires the WHOLE official string, not any exact quote from it

`report._validate_lexicon` (and `find_lexicon_violation`, which shares
the exact same corpus and matcher for GUIDE §6 rule 14a's per-item
correction) exempts a banned-term match only when it falls entirely
inside a **verbatim, complete occurrence of one of three whole strings**
per `_lexicon_officials`: an item's title, an official (non-LLM)
summary, or a BILLACTIONS action sentence — checked in three fixed
forms (`_official_spans`: raw, one-line, display-cased).

Both bills' full titles — "To redesignate the National Historic Trails
Interpretive Center in Casper, Wyoming, as the 'Barbara L. Cubin
National Historic Trails Interpretive Center'." — **are** in that
corpus, and **do** appear verbatim as the item's own heading line,
which is why the heading itself renders "Historic" unmasked without
issue. The map-layer summary — the model's own sentence describing
what the bill does — used the place's name too, but in different
wording ("the National Historic Trails Interpretive Center in Casper,
Wyoming" without the "To redesignate...as the..." wrapper, or similar).
That is not a byte-identical copy of the *whole* title string, so
`_official_spans` finds no exempting span, and the gate — correctly,
under the current rule — treats it as an unattributed use.

The gate's design intent, stated in GUIDE §2 today, is broader than
its implementation: *"Naming the record is stating a fact."* The code
requires naming the **entire sentence** the record used; the principle
only requires naming the **thing** — a name, drawn verbatim from the
published record, used as a label rather than coined by the model.

## The proposed principle (operator's framing, verbatim)

> If a lexicon-gated word is being used to refer to the actual
> published text, then we aren't editorializing, we are simply using a
> label.

Restated as a rule: **a banned term in generated prose is exempt
wherever the exact phrase containing it — at whatever length, down to
the term itself — is drawn verbatim from text the government
published, not text our model composed.** The test is provenance of
the *quoted span*, not completeness of the *quoted sentence*. This
also directly satisfies the standing principle already in GUIDE §2 for
official text generally: *"the ban binds the digest's own voice only."*
A model sentence that reuses three words of an official name has, for
those three words, borrowed the government's voice, not spoken in its
own.

## What changes, precisely

### 1. The corpus of "published text" broadens beyond titles/summaries/action-text

Today `_lexicon_officials` pools: official-method summaries (FR's own
`<SUM>` abstract only — the sole collection with a genuinely
non-LLM summary), every title across every collection and table, and
BILLACTIONS action sentences. It does **not** include the extracted
document **body** (`extracted_texts.text`) — the actual published text
of the bill, opinion, release, or Record entry. A model summary that
quotes a phrase from the body of the document it is summarizing —
which is the single most common and most legitimate way to summarize
something — currently has no exemption path at all unless that same
phrase happens to also be the title.

Proposed: add `extracted_texts.text` (and, where distinct, `granules`
body text if any collection stores it there) to the corpus, scoped to
`digest_day` exactly as the existing sources are. This is squarely
"all published words that were not generated by our LLM" — the
operator's stated bound — and no wider: `summaries.summary` (our own
LLM output, including a *previous* map layer's stored prose) and
`day_summaries`/section-synopsis text are explicitly **excluded**, or
the gate would exempt itself by citing its own prior output.

### 2. The match granularity drops from "whole string" to "exact substring, word-bounded"

This is the change that actually fixes the incident — broadening the
corpus alone does not help, since the mismatch was never about *which*
official strings counted, it was that the model's sentence wasn't a
complete copy of any of them.

Proposed matcher: for every official-text source in the day's corpus
that contains a banned term, do **not** require the render (or, for
`find_lexicon_violation`, the stored item text) to contain that
source's *entire* string. Instead, exempt a banned-term hit in the
render when **some exact, contiguous, word-bounded substring of an
official source — containing that same hit — also occurs verbatim in
the official source at the matching position**. Concretely:
`_official_spans` currently does `scan.find(whole_official_string)`;
the replacement walks outward from every banned-term occurrence
*inside* an official source, in both directions, to the nearest word
boundaries, and registers *that* phrase (not the whole title/body) as
a candidate exempting span, then searches for it in the render exactly
as today. A summary that writes "the National Historic Trails
Interpretive Center" earns the exemption because that four-word phrase
is itself an exact quote from the bill's own title — it does not need
the "To redesignate...Wyoming, as the..." wrapper around it too.

**Minimum span length is a deliberate knob, not an afterthought.**
Exempting on the bare single word alone ("historic" by itself,
divorced from its phrase) would defeat the gate for every item that
happens to share a subject with something that used a banned word
anywhere in its title that day — the opposite of "referring to a
specific label." Proposed floor: the candidate span must include the
full contiguous **proper-noun-shaped phrase** the banned word sits in
per its source occurrence, capped by sentence boundaries, with a hard
minimum of the term plus at least one adjacent word on the side(s)
available (a term at a source's very start/end may only have one
neighbor). "National Historic Trails Interpretive Center" qualifies;
a hypothetical source sentence "...was a **historic** day for the
Senate..." would only exempt "historic day," which is exactly the
right answer — that phrasing is editorializing, occurring in official
text or not, and a model reusing only the bare word without the
specific label it names should still fail. This needs a few worked
examples against real 08-28/08-29 data before the exact boundary
algorithm is locked (see Verification).

### 3. `find_lexicon_violation` (GUIDE §6 rule 14a) shares the identical corpus and matcher

No separate change needed structurally — both functions already call
`_lexicon_officials` and `_official_spans`; fixing the shared helpers
fixes the correction path too. This matters concretely: with the
broadened matcher, H.R. 1693 and H.R. 8121's original map summaries
would very likely have passed the gate on the *first* render, with
**zero** correction calls — the two nights' `map:lexicon-correction`
spend (4 calls, ~147K tokens combined) would not have been needed at
all, because there would have been nothing to correct.

## What does NOT change

- The gate itself, its regex, `config.BANNED_TERMS`, and the
  positional (never global-mask) design are untouched — GUIDE §2's
  core rule stands exactly as written.
- **Our own prose is never exempted by this change.** The corpus
  addition is strictly the extracted **source document** text; a
  model's own summary, plain-language line, Day-in-Review paragraph,
  or section synopsis is never a source of exemption for another
  passage of model prose — that would let the model bootstrap
  editorializing language into legitimacy by citing itself. If a
  future item's summary happens to reuse a phrase from a *different*
  item's LLM-written summary, that reuse is not exempted (LLM output
  is not in the added corpus).
- The r14a correction ceiling, the withdrawal-after-ceiling behavior,
  and the r15 mechanical-floor disclosure are all unchanged — an item
  that still fails after this broadening (a genuine editorializing
  use) is corrected-then-withdrawn exactly as today.
- Compose-level prose (Day in Review, section synopses) stays outside
  `find_lexicon_violation`'s per-item attribution scope, per rule
  14a's existing carve-out; it benefits from the broadened corpus at
  `_validate_lexicon` render time (the whole-day check) but not from a
  targeted correction, unchanged from today.
- No prompt text changes, so **no `PROMPT_VERSION` bump** — this is a
  validation-time and correction-time change only, like the harvest-key
  fix on 2026-08-27 was. (If a future round finds that telling the
  model about the broadened rule up front would reduce first-pass
  trips, that is a separate, later proposal — starting narrow is
  correct here per GUIDE §3a's "prompt changes are deliberate.")

## Risk / blast radius

- **False exemption risk (the gate gets too permissive).** Bounded by
  three things: (a) the corpus is *that day's* published record only —
  not all-time, not other days'; (b) the span must be an *exact*
  contiguous word-bounded quote, never fuzzy/stemmed/case-insensitive
  beyond the existing three display forms; (c) the phrase-boundary
  rule (item 2 above) keeps the exemption tied to the specific named
  thing, not the bare adjective. The single-word banned terms most at
  risk of proper-noun collision ("historic," "landmark," "extreme")
  are exactly the ones this fixes; multi-word idioms ("in an attempt
  to," "aims to appease") are vanishingly unlikely to appear verbatim
  in a government document's own text, so the change is inert for
  them by construction.
- **Performance.** `_official_spans` already only searches sources that
  *themselves* contain a banned term (cheap filter, unchanged);
  extracted document bodies are longer than titles, so the per-source
  scan for banned-term occurrences costs more, but still only for the
  handful of documents per day whose body happens to contain one of 15
  terms — not a per-render cost proportional to corpus size. Measure
  on the dev stack before merging (see Verification).
- **A more permissive gate is a GUIDE-governed change, not a
  code-only one** — hence this document, and hence no implementation
  before your sign-off, per §10 and per CLAUDE.md §13's standing rule
  against loosening a gate to fix a symptom without the operator's
  explicit ruling. This plan treats the ruling above as the mandate,
  pending your confirmation.

## Rollback

Revert the `report.py` matcher change; `_lexicon_officials` and
`_official_spans` return to whole-string-only matching. No schema,
no stored data, and no prompt version is touched, so rollback is a
pure code revert with no data migration.

## Decision requested

1. Approve the principle as stated (exact quote of the published
   record, at phrase granularity, is a label, not editorializing) —
   or narrow/widen it before implementation begins.
2. Approve including `extracted_texts.text` in the exemption corpus
   (the actual document body, not just titles/official summaries/
   action text) — this is the biggest single expansion of what counts
   as "published."
3. Approve the phrase-boundary floor (word-bounded, sentence-capped,
   minimum one adjacent word) as the anti-abuse boundary, or specify a
   different one.
4. Confirm no `PROMPT_VERSION` bump is wanted for this round.
