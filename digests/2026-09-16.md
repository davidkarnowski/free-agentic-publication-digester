## Day in Review

The House recorded two roll call votes: 216-211 on H.J. Res. 210, disapproving an Environmental Protection Agency rule on California nonroad engine pollution control standards for ocean-going vessels at berth, and 220-204 on H. Con. Res. 93, directing the removal of United States Armed Forces from hostilities with Iran under the War Powers Resolution. The chamber also passed measures on elder financial fraud investigations, whistleblower protections for Department of Housing and Urban Development contracts, diesel emissions reduction reauthorization, and personal casualty loss deductions, and adopted a rule for considering five further measures. In the Senate, the Byrne nomination was confirmed 52-45, cloture was invoked 74-24 on the motion to proceed to S. 4668 on student-athlete name, image, and likeness rights, and a cloture motion on H.R. 3633, the digital commodities bill, was rejected 49-50. The digest carries 21 House-engrossed bills, 16 reported bills, 11 introduced, and one enrolled law, the Modernizing Access to Our Public Oceans Act.

From the executive branch, the digest carries eight final rules, three proposed rules, 92 notices, and one presidential document continuing the terrorism-related national emergency declared in 2001. Final rules include Labor's unemployment compensation data disclosure rule, a Commodity Futures Trading Commission whistleblower award amendment, and Coast Guard safety zones. Two executive orders, one memorandum, and two proclamations appear, covering Chesapeake Bay water quality, federal procurement of Canadian-origin items, Constitution Week, and National POW/MIA Recognition Day.

The digest carries 63 appellate opinions. The Fifth Circuit vacated the Environmental Protection Agency's methylene chloride rule and risk determination under the Toxic Substances Control Act, and reversed Pullman abstention in an Austin taxation case. The Tenth Circuit reversed a district court ruling that had barred filling the expanded Gross Reservoir, concluding the Army Corps properly issued its permit. The First Circuit affirmed public access to civil proceedings in a Maine lottery winner's case; the Ninth Circuit vacated a denial of qualified immunity to four Anchorage officers; and the Eleventh Circuit affirmed convictions under the Maritime Drug Law Enforcement Act.

## Terms Used Today

- *cloture* — a Senate vote to end debate so a final vote can happen
- *concurrent resolution* — a measure passed by both chambers that does not go to the President and does not have the force of law
- *engrossed* — the official text of a bill as passed by one chamber
- *enrolled* — the final text of a bill passed by both chambers, sent to the President
- *interim final rule* — a rule that takes effect without waiting for public comment, though comments are still accepted
- *joint resolution* — a measure that, like a bill, becomes law if passed and signed
- *motion to proceed* — a Senate vote on whether to start considering a bill
- *notice of proposed rulemaking* — the formal announcement of a draft regulation
- *proposed rule* — a draft regulation published for public comment before adoption
- *safety zone* — a temporary area of water that vessels may not enter without permission
