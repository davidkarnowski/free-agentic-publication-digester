The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, part of the Department of Transportation, sets vehicle-safety standards and orders recalls and publishes press releases on safety regulations, recalls, defect investigations, and crash data.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) is a federal agency within the Department of Transportation responsible for reducing deaths and injuries from motor vehicle crashes. NHTSA has two primary regulatory authorities: setting and enforcing vehicle-safety standards that manufacturers must meet, and administering the recall program for vehicles with safety defects. The agency also conducts research on vehicle safety, collects and analyzes crash data, investigates vehicle defects and complaints, and operates a public database of safety information.

NHTSA's press-release index is the agency's official communications channel. Releases typically appear a few times per week and cover various aspects of vehicle safety regulation. Announcements address recall orders when NHTSA identifies safety defects in vehicles, investigations into potential defects, new or updated safety standards for manufacturers to follow, research findings about vehicle safety performance, recalls issued in coordination with manufacturers, enforcement actions against manufacturers, data releases from NHTSA's crash databases, consumer safety guidance, and child safety seat regulations. The releases communicate directly with vehicle manufacturers, dealerships, consumers, and safety advocates.

NHTSA releases serve as official documentation of vehicle-safety decisions and regulatory actions. In the digest, these announcements represent the public record of vehicle safety regulations affecting millions of vehicles in operation. Readers encounter official notice of recalls requiring owner action, new safety standards manufacturers must meet, and research findings about vehicle safety. The releases provide straightforward information about which vehicles are subject to safety recalls, what defects are being addressed, what corrective actions are available, and what safety standards and regulations the agency is implementing or enforcing.
