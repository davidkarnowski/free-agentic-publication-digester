**Model-written orientation**

The U.S. Sentencing Commission, an independent judicial branch agency, publishes releases about federal sentencing guidelines, research findings, and public proceedings.

The United States Sentencing Commission is an independent agency established by statute within the judicial branch of the federal government. The Commission has a core responsibility to develop and maintain the Federal Sentencing Guidelines—a system of rules that guides federal judges in sentencing individuals convicted of federal crimes.

The Commission is structured to represent different perspectives on sentencing policy. Its membership includes federal judges, prosecutors, defense attorneys representing both public defenders and private practice, senior law enforcement officials, and other relevant stakeholders. This diverse composition reflects the Commission's role as an independent policy-making body within the judicial branch.

Beyond developing and updating the sentencing guidelines, the Commission conducts research on sentencing outcomes and trends, publishes statistical reports and empirical studies on federal sentencing, and holds public hearings where interested parties can present views on proposed guideline amendments.

The Commission's news feed publishes official releases and announcements about its work and activities. Releases typically cover proposed guideline amendments and amendments that have been adopted, notices of public hearings and meetings the Commission is conducting, statistical and research reports the Commission publishes or completes, information about the Commission's public meetings and rule-making proceedings, and other announcements related to the Commission's official activities.

The feed typically produces a few items per month, though publication frequency may vary depending on the Commission's schedule and activities. This source is valuable for readers interested in understanding how federal sentencing policy is developed and refined, what research and data inform sentencing decisions, and how the federal sentencing system evolves over time.
