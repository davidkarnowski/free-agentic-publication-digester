The U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO), within the Commerce Department, publishes news on intellectual-property policy, examination operations, and regulatory matters.

The U.S. Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) is the federal agency within the Department of Commerce responsible for granting patents and registering trademarks. Established in its current form in 1975, the USPTO administers the nation's patent and trademark systems, examining applications, issuing patents, registering trademarks, and setting policy governing intellectual property rights in the United States.

The USPTO's news-updates channel publishes press releases and formal notices related to the agency's operations and policies. Content includes announcements of new fee schedules or policy changes affecting patent or trademark examination, notices of procedural updates, releases about systems improvements or modernization efforts, organizational announcements, statistical reports on application filings and patents issued, and notices of public comment periods for proposed rule changes. Updates are typically published several times per week.

Items appearing in this digest from the USPTO will reflect official announcements the agency has published. These may include changes to patent or trademark application fees, updates to examination procedures or requirements, announcements of new examination tools or systems, patent office performance statistics, procedural guidance to patent practitioners and applicants, updates to trademark searchability or registration processes, or organizational changes. The USPTO's announcements affect inventors, patent attorneys, trademark owners, businesses, and anyone seeking intellectual property protection.

The USPTO's news channel serves as the authoritative source for policy changes and operational updates affecting the patent and trademark systems. Understanding what the USPTO announces helps stakeholders stay current with changes to examination standards, fees, procedures, and systems that shape how intellectual property is protected in the United States.
