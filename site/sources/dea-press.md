**Model-written orientation**

The Drug Enforcement Administration is the Justice Department's principal agency for enforcing federal controlled-substances law.

The Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) is a bureau of the Department of Justice and is the primary federal agency responsible for enforcing the Controlled Substances Act and related laws regarding illegal drugs, chemical precursors, and the diversion of controlled medications. The DEA operates through a network of field divisions in major cities and maintains offices in foreign countries, investigating drug trafficking organizations, manufacturing operations, and distribution networks.

The DEA's functions include investigating federal drug crimes, regulating the legal manufacture and distribution of controlled substances through a licensing system, preventing the diversion of chemical precursors used to manufacture illegal drugs, providing training and support to state and local law enforcement agencies, and conducting international drug control efforts. The agency also maintains the drug scheduling system, under which new drugs or drug analogs are evaluated and placed into one of five schedules based on their abuse potential and medical utility, and issues alerts on emerging drug threats.

DEA press releases typically cover major drug trafficking investigations and indictments, drug seizures, dismantling of clandestine laboratories, product recalls of controlled medications, drug-scheduling actions, law enforcement operations, training initiatives, and public alerts on dangerous drugs or products. The releases document the agency's enforcement activities and policy actions.
