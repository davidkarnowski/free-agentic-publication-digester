The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission is an independent commission that regulates wholesale electricity markets, interstate natural gas pipelines, and hydropower licensing.

The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) is an independent agency within the federal government responsible for regulating critical infrastructure sectors: wholesale electricity markets, interstate natural gas pipelines, and hydroelectric facilities. The Commission operates with regulatory authority to set rates, review applications, and issue orders in proceedings involving utilities, generators, pipeline companies, and other market participants. FERC's regulatory mandate ensures that rates for wholesale electricity and natural gas transportation are just and reasonable, that hydroelectric projects are licensed and operated safely and efficiently, and that interstate energy markets operate reliably and competitively. The Commission comprises five members and operates with quasi-judicial authority to hold hearings, consider evidence, and issue orders in response to applications, complaints, and other matters brought before it. The agency's public news releases reflect the regular flow of its regulatory work, which includes orders issued in proceedings on specific applications or disputes, announcements of proposed rulemakings that establish new policies or procedures, notices of monthly open meetings where commissioners deliberate and vote on matters, and procedural announcements concerning its dockets. Readers encountering FERC materials in this digest will see orders addressing applications from utilities or pipeline operators, decisions in disputes over rates or service terms, announcements of new proposed regulations, meeting notices and summaries, and guidance documents clarifying FERC policies or procedures. The materials reveal the deliberative, documented nature of federal energy regulation, with decisions made through formal proceedings that include public participation and written orders explaining the Commission's reasoning. FERC's published announcements document its ongoing regulatory role in the energy sector, covering electricity markets, natural gas transportation networks, and hydroelectric licensing and relicensing proceedings. The Commission's independence means that its regulatory positions and decisions reflect its own statutory authority and judgment, not guidance from other agencies or political branches.
