## Day in Review

The digest carries 43 appellate opinions, 960 district court opinions, five bankruptcy court opinions and one national court item, along with eight agency press releases. Among the appellate items, the Ninth Circuit issued a published opinion reversing the District of Arizona's judgment on a conspiracy count in United States v. Abdul Kareem. That count charged conspiracy to provide material support to ISIS, and the panel remanded with instructions to enter a judgment of acquittal on it and to resentence. In a published opinion, the Sixth Circuit affirmed Gerald Cook, Jr.'s 248-month sentence on gun and drug charges.

The Tenth Circuit reversed a District of Colorado preliminary injunction that had ordered the NCAA to allow a group of student-athletes a fifth season. It also reversed summary judgment for an employer in an EEOC disability suit. The Eleventh Circuit vacated its April 14, 2026 opinion in McClinton v. Warden and substituted a revised opinion that deletes one footnote and again affirms qualified immunity. The Federal Circuit affirmed dismissal of a former Forest Service employee's severance claim. The Fifth Circuit affirmed several supervised-release revocations, rejecting challenges under United States v. Haymond that the defendants raised for the first time on appeal. The national court item is a notice of entry and service of a sealed document in Yantai Oriental Protein Tech Co. v. United States.

*Inference (GUIDE §6 r15, standing): The pipeline finalizes every
publication day with or without an inference provider. Model layers
are additive. When no inference was available for a day, the digest
states that fact in its own prose and nothing more; the cause is
operational detail recorded in the day's provenance and operations
report, not in the published digest. The Coverage Statement's
arithmetic reconciles regardless. Items are listed with their
citations whether or not they were summarized. A day finalized
without model layers is frozen like any other day; prose is not
backfilled into a frozen digest.*
