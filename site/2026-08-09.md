#### United States Court of International Trade

- **Kemper AIP Metals, LLC et al v. United States** (No. 1:26-cv-00762; filed 2026-08-07) — The U.S. Court of International Trade granted motions to intervene by Nucor Corporation, Steel Dynamics Inc., U.S. Steel Corporation, and the United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers International Union in a case challenging the Department of Commerce's antidumping duty determination on corrosion-resistant steel products from Brazil. The court found the intervenors have constitutional standing and qualify as interested parties under 28 U.S.C. § 2631(j)(1)(B) because they seek only the same relief as the United States—affirmance of Commerce's final determination. The determination, issued in December 2025, found certain Brazilian corrosion-resistant steel products were being sold at less than fair value and imposed corresponding antidumping duties.
  - *In plain terms:* Steel manufacturers and a union were allowed to participate in a lawsuit about the government's decision to impose import duties on corrosion-resistant steel from Brazil.
  - Included because: USCOURTS-SEL-02 — national court opinion (all listed) (document dated 2026-08-07)
  - Source: [USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00762 / USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00762-0](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00762/USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00762-0)
- **Kemper AIP Metals, LLC et al v. United States** (No. 1:26-cv-00764; filed 2026-08-07) — The U.S. Court of International Trade granted motions to intervene by Cleveland-Cliffs Inc., Nucor Corporation, Steel Dynamics Inc., U.S. Steel Corporation, and the United Steel, Paper and Forestry, Rubber, Manufacturing, Energy, Allied Industrial and Service Workers International Union in a case challenging the U.S. International Trade Commission's affirmative material injury determination regarding corrosion-resistant steel products from Australia, Brazil, Canada, Mexico, the Netherlands, South Africa, Taiwan, Turkey, the United Arab Emirates, and Vietnam. The court found the intervenors have constitutional standing and qualify as interested parties under 28 U.S.C. § 2631(j)(1)(B) because they manufacture the subject merchandise and seek only affirmance of the Commission's determination. The Commission's November 2025 determination and the subsequent December 2025 antidumping duty orders concluded that corrosion-resistant steel imports caused material injury to the domestic industry.
  - *In plain terms:* Steel manufacturers were allowed to participate in a case about whether corrosion-resistant steel imports from multiple countries caused harm to the U.S. industry.
  - Included because: USCOURTS-SEL-02 — national court opinion (all listed) (document dated 2026-08-07)
  - Source: [USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00764 / USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00764-0](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00764/USCOURTS-cit-1_26-cv-00764-0)
