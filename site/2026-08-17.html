ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION AGENCY

  • Calcium Carbonate; Exemption From the Requirement of a Pesticide Tolerance (2026-16720; 40 CFR Part 180) — This regulation establishes an exemption from the requirement of a tolerance for residues of calcium carbonate (CAS # 471-34-1), in or on all food commodities when used in accordance with label directions and good agricultural practices. Columbia River Carbonates, submitted a petition to EPA under the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FFDCA), requesting an exemption from the requirement of a tolerance. This regulation eliminates the need to establish a maximum permissible level for residues of calcium carbonate in or on all food commodities, in accordance with the terms of the exemption. Action: Final rule. Dates: This regulation is effective August 17, 2026. Objections and requests for hearings must be received on or before October 16, 2026, and must be filed in accordance with the instructions provided in 40 CFR part 178 (see also Unit I.C. of this document).
  • In plain terms This rule establishes an exemption from the requirement to set a maximum permissible level for calcium carbonate residues on all food commodities, when used as directed, following a petition from Columbia River Carbonates.
  • Included because: FR-SEL-01 — document type: final rule (all listed)

  • Proposed Removal of a Reporting Requirement for Trusts Whose Charitable Contribution Deductions Are Solely for Contributions Made by Passthrough Entities (2026-16769; 26 CFR Part 1) — This document contains proposed regulations that would amend existing regulations that require certain trusts to report all charitable contributions and amounts permanently set aside for a charitable purpose on Form 1041-A, U.S. Information Return Trust Accumulation of Charitable Amounts. The proposed regulations would remove the reporting requirement for these trusts with respect to taxable years in which the trust's only claimed charitable contribution deduction results from charitable contributions made by a passthrough entity in which the trust owns an interest. The proposed regulations would also modify the existing regulations to clarify that split-interest trusts satisfy their filing obligations by filing Form 5227, Split-Interest Trust Information Return, rather than Form 1041-A. The proposed regulations would affect certain trusts that are required to report all charitable contributions and amounts permanently set aside for a charitable purpose. Action: Notice of proposed rulemaking. Dates: Written or electronic comments and requests for a public hearing must be received by October 16, 2026.
  • In plain terms Proposed regulations would amend rules for trusts reporting charitable contributions, removing the Form 1041-A requirement for trusts whose only deductions come from passthrough entities, and clarifying that split-interest trusts file Form 5227.
